# Permanent Channel Bible

Channel language: Hindi. Primary focus: India, including Bollywood and regional Indian cinema.
Major global developments are information alerts until the human explicitly selects a story.
Aim for under five minutes where accuracy and clarity allow. No filler.
Style: cinematic documentary explainer with consistent 2D anime-inspired original illustrations.
Use an original fictional female illustrated anchor, female Hindi narration, and the approved permanent anchor reference and newsroom design from the Claude project's channel files.
Do not imply the fictional anchor is a real journalist or public figure.
Keep source links and claim-to-source references in research notes and the proposed video description. Preserve uncertainty and the distinction between claims and established facts.
Narration should flow naturally. Do not routinely say "according to BBC", "this website reports", or read publisher names and URLs aloud in every video or after every fact.
Mention a source in narration only when its identity is essential to understanding or evaluating the claim: a person's tweet/post or public statement, a direct quotation, an allegation, an exclusive or uncorroborated report, disputed information, or a materially uncertain estimate. Identify the actual speaker/account/authority where known and relevant, rather than mechanically naming an outlet that repeated it.
For a tweet or public post, make clear who posted it and what they stated; do not present the post itself as proof that the underlying claim is true. If the original post has not been verified, say so when material.
Routine independently established background facts need no repetitive spoken source mention. Use concise on-screen attribution only where needed for context, uncertainty, licensing, or a graphic; retain the full evidence trail outside the narration. Never remove essential attribution in a way that makes a claim misleading or falsely suggests original reporting.
Never portray generated illustrations as authentic news photos or footage.
Use original or properly licensed assets. Film footage, posters, music, and photographs require appropriate permission or another valid basis for use.
Human approval is required at research, Hindi script, storyboard/visual plan, and final video review stages.
Publishing always requires separate explicit authorization.

## Studio handoff
The discovery studio supplies two text outputs: a news-story prompt and a story-specific reference-picture prompt. The user copies them into a separate Claude video agent, which handles video production and its approvals. A picture prompt describes the desired illustration; it is not an attached image. The studio does not render pictures, generate voice, make videos or publish them.

## Original-story workflow
Treat publisher feeds as private discovery leads. Select only stories with meaningful public interest and sufficient evidence for further production research. Research important claims using original statements, documents and independent reporting before treating them as established facts.
Develop our own story structure, Hindi narration, explanation and analysis. Do not copy a publisher's script or rewrite it sentence by sentence. News links do not grant permission to reuse articles, footage, film clips, photos or music; use original illustrations and properly licensed assets.
For headline-only records, state in the production research review that article text was not collected and further research is required. Do not turn a headline or repeated coverage into a confirmed fact.
The human selects the story and approves production stages. Publishing remains a separate explicitly authorized action.

## Permanent anchor and environment direction
Treat the approved anchor and newsroom files in the user's Claude project as the visual source of truth for every episode. Reuse the same assets where possible; a text description alone does not guarantee visual identity.
Keep the anchor's face, age appearance, skin tone, hairstyle, facial hair, body proportions, outfit, colours and line style consistent with the approved reference. Do not substitute a celebrity or redesign the anchor for each story. Use a calm, credible, conversational Hindi delivery, clear pronunciation and restrained expressions. Avoid shouting, exaggerated reactions and repetitive greetings.
Keep the newsroom's layout, desk, screen positions, camera axis, lighting and base palette consistent with its approved reference. Change only the story graphics on the screens. Never invent channel branding or present a generated background as a real location.

## Story representation and pacing
Choose the structure to suit the evidence: a clear factual hook, what is established, essential background, timeline or explanation, why it matters to the audience, and what remains unknown or happens next. A short story may combine these beats. Do not manufacture suspense, certainty or a dramatic ending.
Use the anchor for a concise introduction, difficult explanations and the closing takeaway; let story illustrations, maps, timelines and graphics carry most of the explanation. Avoid keeping a static anchor on screen for the whole video. Start with roughly 15–25 percent anchor time and adjust for clarity; this is a flexible planning target.
Use natural Hindi with short sentences, explain unfamiliar terms, and distinguish established facts, attributed claims and analysis. Sensitive events need restrained visuals. Entertainment stories can be livelier while preserving factual and numerical caveats.
Plan purposeful visual changes, usually every 5–10 seconds, while giving charts and Hindi captions enough reading time. Avoid motion or cuts solely to fill time. Captions should be short, legible in Devanagari and away from the face and important evidence. Do not place a new text overlay over every sentence.

## Default 2D animation language
Use a hybrid of layered 2D cut-out animation and motion graphics. Separate characters, foreground, background and overlays into reusable layers. Use modest camera pans/zooms, restrained parallax, pose changes, blinks, head nods and simple hand gestures. Preserve the illustrated look; do not drift into photorealistic or unrelated 3D styles.
Choose scene types deliberately: anchor presentation for explanation; layered contextual illustrations for setting; animated timelines for sequence; maps for verified geography; charts for verified comparable numbers; and quote/post cards only for verified wording with essential attribution. Do not fabricate screenshots or documentary evidence.
Use prepared mouth shapes with audio-driven timing only when a usable anchor rig and voice track exist. Otherwise use an honest limited-animation treatment with voiceover, occasional poses and cutaways; do not claim accurate lip-sync. Full frame-by-frame character animation and AI image-to-video are optional exceptions, not the default. Ask before introducing a new paid service.
Prefer a reusable Remotion project when the downstream Claude agent has code execution and rendering tools. Keep anchor, newsroom, type, palette and transitions as shared components. Reuse the same actual assets across videos. If another animation tool is already configured, retain the same visual rules. A normal chat session without rendering tools should provide the script, storyboard and implementation instructions, not pretend it exported an MP4.
Default delivery target: 1920x1080, 16:9, 30 fps, with the final voiceover driving timing. Prefer simple cuts, short dissolves and purposeful graphic transitions. Use restrained properly licensed sound; keep narration intelligible. Verify image/voice tools, rights, available renderer and any paid costs before production; do not assume a subscription includes all of them.
